Chapter 01 · Start here

The platform in one picture

The platform is Algeria's single entry point to the Social and Solidarity Economy (ESS): a public site that informs, member spaces where people work together, and a back-office where programme staff decide and publish.

The idea

Behind all the screens there is one main pipeline. An entrepreneur submits a project, programme staff point it to the right stage of the journey, the entrepreneur builds a file of documents, staff approve it, and the project is matched with an accredited coach. When the stage is done, the next one opens, until the project reaches financing and is closed.

Everything else either feeds that pipeline or supports it. Citizens volunteer, mentor or pledge donations to published projects. The Académie trains entrepreneurs and citizens. Content pages, news and resources inform the public. Statistics let the programme report to its funder, the European Union.

Deliberately out of scope

  • No money moves through the platform. Donation “pledges” are promises only.
  • No native mobile apps, only a responsive website.
  • No live video hosting. The platform stores the link to an external tool.
  • No data exchange with ANADE, ANEM, ANGEM, NESDA or CNAC yet.
  • No forums or public social features, and no file attachments in messages.

Also out of scope

  • No per-user notification preferences. Super-admins set notifications for the whole platform.
  • No automatic translation. The client supplies the Arabic content.
  • Newsletters are written and sent outside the platform, which only collects subscribers.
  • No role for funders or businesses yet.
  • No interactive map of support structures (a categorised list instead, Q17).

Glossary & synonyms

Yes, the “coach” is the Accompagnateur·rice. The tech blueprint already uses coach as its code name. But the SRS has three different people who “help” a project, and mixing them up will break the data model.

Accompagnateur·rice = coach

A support professional or structure (incubator, accelerator, agency, network). Must hold an accreditation (agrément) valid 2 years. Has a capacity of places set by admins. Sees the private dossier once a coaching relation is accepted.

FR-36 → FR-41

Mentor = a Citizen

A member of the public offering mentoring (« Mentorat ») as a contribution, next to volunteering and donation pledges. Not vetted (Q3), has no capacity and never sees the dossier. The Entrepreneur accepts or refuses the offer.

FR-23, FR-43

Formateur·rice = trainer

Teaches trainings in the Académie. Must pass a qualification test and be validated. Has no link to any specific project. Learners are Entrepreneurs, Citizens or guests.

FR-46 → FR-49

False friends: words the SRS uses for more than one thing

  • Four kinds of “stage”. These are four different fields, so keep them apart in the ERD:
    • Statut (status) is the workflow position, e.g. oriente.
    • Phase (also called « parcours », and shown as « Étape » in the Mes projets list) is one of the 4 journey steps.
    • Stade d'avancement is the public stage shown on the catalogue: Idéation, Conception, Financement, Développement.
    • Étape 1 sur 4 is the step number in the submission wizard.
  • « Proposition » means both a coach's proposal to accompany (FR-39) and a Citizen's offer (FR-43, e.g. « Proposition de bénévolat reçue »).
  • « Retirer » (withdraw/remove) has five meanings:
    • the Entrepreneur withdraws a project, which is final;
    • an admin removes a training from the catalogue;
    • a document is removed before the dossier is submitted;
    • a kit document or an announcement is hidden;
    • a team member is removed.
  • « Terminé » applies to:
    • a project, when all its phases are done;
    • an accompaniment, when the coach closes it;
    • a contribution, when either party closes it;
    • a training in a learner's list, when the quiz is passed.
  • One status, several labels. The same state is shown differently depending on who looks:
    • the project status soumis reads « En examen » to the Entrepreneur and « Soumis — à examiner » in the back-office;
    • a training the admin sees as « Refusée » reads « À revoir » to the trainer.
    Store one key and map the labels per audience.
  • « Extranet » in the admin scope names (« Contenu de l'Extranet ») means the public portal's content, not a private area.
  • Structure vs Accompagnateur. A structure is a directory entry with no login. An accompagnateur is an account, which can be of type « Structure » and can be linked to a directory structure.

Check yourself

A retired engineer wants to guide Amina on pricing, a few hours a month. Coach or mentor?

A mentor: a Citizen account sending a « Mentorat » offer. A coach is a professional who needs an accreditation, and that person isn't one.

The catalogue chip says “Conception” and Amina's « Étape » column says “Incubation”. Is that a contradiction?

No. The first is the public stage, set by the admin when publishing. The second is the phase of the journey. They are two different fields. How public stages map to phases is open (Q1, Q2).

“Incubateur de Ghardaïa” appears in the directory. Can it receive applications?

No. A directory structure has no account. Only an accredited Accompagnateur account receives applications. Such an account can be linked to that structure, and the structure page then lists it.

The cast: who uses the platform

There are eight roles. Four of them sign up themselves, and each signs up through a different “gate”. Administrators are invited, and the two super-admins exist from installation.

The idea

A role is chosen once, at registration, and it decides the starting status and the gate the person has to pass before they can do their job. Entrepreneurs and Citizens pass no gate. A coach needs an accreditation. A trainer needs a qualification test plus an admin validation. Everything a person can see is then checked on the server against four things: their role, their account status, their admin scope (for admins) and whether they own the item (SRS 5.2).

Visitor

Anyone browsing

No account. Reads published content, searches, subscribes to the newsletter, uses the contact form, answers anonymous surveys and registers for events. Can also register for in-person and hybrid training sessions without an account (pending, Q5).

FR-06 → FR-30

Entrepreneur

Porteur·se de projet

Person or ESS organisation carrying a project. Active immediately. Creates, submits, edits, withdraws and transfers projects. Uploads the dossier and invites a team. Applies to shortlisted coaches and answers their proposals. Decides on citizen offers and takes trainings.

Gate: a verified e-mail before submitting anything · FR-31 → FR-35

Team member

Invited co-founder

An Entrepreneur account invited onto someone else's project. Can view every tab and take part in the project's conversations. Cannot submit, delete or change the team. Becomes the owner if the owner transfers the project.

Max 10 per project · FR-35

Accompagnateur (coach)

Support professional or structure

Starts « Inactif » and requests the accreditation. Once accredited (2 years) the coach can:

  • see oriented projects in their phases;
  • propose to accompany one;
  • accept or refuse applications;
  • coach the project, close the accompaniment with an outcome, or ask to end it early.

Capacity is set by admins.

Gate: accreditation · FR-36 → FR-41

Trainer

Formateur·rice

Registers with a CV and starts « Qualification à passer ». Follows the qualification course and passes the test. Can then author trainings and submit them for validation. Gets the pedagogical kit only once an admin validates the account.

Gate: test + validation · FR-46 → FR-49

Citizen

Citoyen·ne

Active immediately. Browses projects and offers volunteering, mentoring or a donation pledge. Either party can close an accepted contribution. Answers nominative surveys and takes trainings. Can message an Entrepreneur once an offer is accepted.

FR-42, FR-43

Administrator

Programme staff

Invited by a super-admin with a provisional password. Works only inside the scopes assigned, out of 7. Covers moderation, orientation, shortlists, content, surveys, announcements, contact inbox, statistics.

FR-53 → FR-71

Super-administrator

Platform owner (exactly 2)

One account for PNUD, one for the national institution. Everything an admin can do, in every scope. On top of that:

  • invite, scope and suspend admins;
  • reference lists and platform settings;
  • the full activity journal;
  • change a user's e-mail after an identity check;
  • restore the other super-admin's access.

FR-55, FR-56, FR-69b, FR-69c

Rules about roles that shape the data model

  • One e-mail = one account = one profile. The user can't change the profile chosen at registration. Someone who is both a coach and a trainer needs two accounts with two addresses FR-01.
  • “Learner” is not a role. Entrepreneurs and Citizens use the learning space. Guests can register for in-person sessions without an account FR-20, FR-44.
  • Team member is not a role either. It is an Entrepreneur linked to a project they don't own FR-35.
  • The demo's role picker and « Changer de vue » switcher must not exist in the product. The role comes from the account FR-02.
  • Admins are left out of every user count, can't use “Se souvenir de moi”, and need stronger passwords (12+ characters, 3 of 4 character families) FR-02, FR-03, FR-53.

Chapter 06 · Core journey

The project journey

A project is a file that moves through statuses. The Entrepreneur writes and submits it. An administrator decides at two checkpoints: orientation (which phase?) and dossier validation (is the file good?). Approval unlocks coaching, and each phase is one loop of the same cycle.

The idea

Think of an admission desk followed by a ladder. At the desk (soumis), an admin decides where the project enters the ladder, which is one of four phases. On each rung the Entrepreneur collects the documents that phase requires (oriente) and hands them in (dossier_soumis). The admin then accepts the rung (approuve), sends documents back for correction, or refuses. Once a rung is accepted, a coach helps the project through it. When the coach says the phase is done, the admin either opens the next rung or closes the project (termine).

Two other things run alongside, separate from the status: whether the project is published on the public site (an admin choice, FR-60), and who is in its team (FR-35).

  4. Besoins: the needs, the coaching wish, the writing language, and a recap of every answer.

Accompaniment matching

Coaches must be accredited and have limited places. A project meets a coach through one of two doors, and both lead to the same accepted relation, which later ends with an outcome.

The idea

Door 1, the coach proposes. As soon as a project is oriented to a phase the coach is accredited for, the coach can see it and offer help. This exists so that a coach can help build the dossier.

Door 2, the Entrepreneur applies. Only after the dossier is approved, and only to coaches an admin shortlisted, by answering a questionnaire.

Either way, once accepted: a shared conversation opens, the coach can read the dossier, and one of the coach's places is used. The relation ends normally when the coach closes it with an outcome. It ends early when an admin approves a request to end it, or automatically if the project is refused, re-oriented or withdrawn.

The Académie

Trainers qualify, author trainings, and get them validated. Learners register to sessions or follow online lessons and pass quizzes. Admins moderate, and can also publish trainings directly.

The idea

It works like a small marketplace with quality control. A trainer can't publish anything until passing the qualification, and every training a trainer writes goes through admin validation. Admins can skip that step for their own catalogue trainings. A training comes in one of three formats. Online means lessons and a quiz. In person means dated sessions with limited places. Hybrid means both, and each session can be attended on site (limited) or live remotely (unlimited). Full sessions have a waiting list, and freed places are offered in turn.

Chapter 12 · Platform services

Back-office & governance

Admins work from queues, and each admin sees only the queues of their scopes. Public content goes through a two-person review. Every decision is recorded.

The idea

The back-office is organised around work queues: everything waiting for a human decision, oldest first, flagged « En retard » after a configurable delay. Chapter 13 · Reference

Rules that apply everywhere

These rules hold on every screen. Learn them once and you'll recognise them in every FR.

Published only

Anything public shows published items only: lists, homepage, search, related items, sitemap. An unknown or unpublished item returns a 404. The demo leaked drafts, the product must not.

Two languages, one format

Every translatable field is stored in French and Arabic separately. Missing Arabic falls back to French with a notice. Projects are shown in the language they were written in. Dates are DD/MM/YYYY, times 24 h in Algeria time (UTC+1), and digits are Western in both languages (1 254).

A reason for every “no”

Every refusal, suspension or cancellation needs a reason of 10–500 characters. The person concerned sees it, and it is recorded in the history.

No money

No payment anywhere. Donation pledges are declared amounts, labelled “non collectées” in statistics. Citizen financing in the panorama is information only.

Atomic counters

Four counters must never oversell: coach places, on-site session places (shared by guests and members), event places, and survey quotas. When two people race for the last unit, the second gets a “complet” message.

One click, one record

Double clicks record a single action: project submission, “Postuler”, “Clôturer”, decisions. Two people editing the same thing never overwrite each other silently (“modifié par {nom} à HH:MM”). A decision taken twice shows “déjà traité par {nom}”.

Undo has conditions

Accepting an application can be undone for 10 s. “Revenir sur la décision” works only while the other party hasn't acted (projects) or no learner has registered (trainings). A refusal can be contested once.

Reminders, not expiry

Applications, proposals and offers never expire. They trigger reminders after 7, 7 and 14 days. What does expire: e-mail links, codes, invitations, waiting-list offers (48 h), edit suggestions (30 days), accreditations (2 years).

Clocks start at delivery

When the mail queue is slow, an e-mail link's validity (24 h, 60 min, 5 min, 7 days…) counts from actual delivery, not from the click that requested it.

Files are suspicious

The type is checked on content, not only on extension. Every file is scanned for malware. While the scanner is down, a file is quarantined and only its uploader sees it. Private files (dossiers, proofs, CVs, kit, lessons) are served through links that check authorisation on every request.

Checked on the server, every time

Role, account status, admin scope and ownership are checked on every request. Anything else gets “Accès réservé” (HTTP 403). Checks done only in the browser, as in the demo, don't count.

Every list has four states

Loading, empty (with an explanatory message), error (with “Réessayer”), and access-restricted. No screen ships without them.

Logged and notified

Every significant action goes into the history. Every decision notifies the person concerned, on the channels set for that notification type.

Delete means anonymise

When an account is deleted:

  • a project with a team passes to a member; otherwise it is unpublished and anonymised;
  • messages show « Compte supprimé »;
  • decisions are kept without the person's name.

Inactive accounts are anonymised after 3 years (Q20).

One source of truth

Key figures are stored once. Needs are a single field. Filters and forms share the reference lists, so every filter option can match. Values typed under “Autre (préciser)” are grouped under “Autre”.

One key, many labels

A status has one stored key and a label per audience (Entrepreneur, back-office, trainer). The French interface uses inclusive writing (porteur·se, accompagnateur·rice).

Hosted in Algeria

The platform, its data, backups and logs stay in Algeria. Backups run daily, encrypted, and are kept 30 days. At most 24 h of data can be lost, and a restore takes at most 8 h. Availability is 99.5 % a month. At handover, a full export in an open format (SQL dump and files).

Performance budget

Pages load in 3 s on 4G. Filters respond in 1 s on lists of up to 10,000 items, and search in 2 s for 95 % of queries. 200 concurrent users, the pilot size. Exports, PDFs, announcements, e-mails and videos run in a background queue.
